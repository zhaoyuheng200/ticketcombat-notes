# Why there's no download of the original site

**Short answer: the author's terms don't allow it.**

It would take a few minutes to bundle every archived page of TicketCombat.com
into one PDF or ZIP file and put a download button right here. The site is
offline, the Wayback Machine is slow, and a single file would be far more
convenient. It hasn't been done, **on purpose**. This page explains why.

## What the site's terms say

TicketCombat.com's [Terms and Conditions](https://web.archive.org/web/20251021000000/https://www.ticketcombat.com/terms.php)
(last updated March 16, 2011) say:

> You may display on your computer, download and print a copy of the Content
> for non-commercial, personal or educational purposes only, provided that the
> content is not modified and that each copy of such materials identifies the
> source URL of the webpage address. However, no part of any document on the
> Site may be used, reproduced, broadcast, published, downloaded, uploaded or
> transmitted for any other purpose.

and:

> Except as expressly provided in these Terms and Conditions of Use, any
> reproduction, retransmission, distribution, sale, republication,
> modification, translation of, or creation of derivative works based on the
> website or its content, in whole or in part, … without prior written
> permission, is strictly prohibited.

In plain terms: **you may keep a copy for yourself, but you may not hand copies
out.** Offering a PDF on a public website is distribution, whatever the file
format.

## "But the site is gone"

Offline isn't the same as abandoned, and it doesn't make the content free to
copy:

- **Copyright doesn't expire when hosting does.** In Canada it lasts for the
  author's lifetime plus 70 years, whether or not the site is still up.
- **The author still seems to be around.** The domain was renewed in December
  2025 and is paid through January 2027 (see [History](history.md)). The
  hosting lapsed, not the ownership.
- **So the decision is still theirs.** Republishing their work without asking
  would ignore the one thing they clearly wrote down about it.

## What you can do instead

1. **Read the original on the Wayback Machine.** Every page is linked from its
   section summary and from the [page index](page-index.md). It's slow, but it's
   the legitimate public copy. Open links in a new tab and give them a moment.
   If one capture fails, try again or pick another date on the Wayback calendar.
2. **Keep your own personal copy.** The terms explicitly allow you to download
   and print a copy for your own non-commercial use, unmodified and showing
   where it came from. Your browser's **Print → Save as PDF** on an archived
   page does exactly that; the Wayback address on the printout identifies the
   source. Keep it for yourself, and don't share it.
3. **Use these notes for the substance.** Each section summary covers what the
   page argued, the law it cited, and what has changed since.
