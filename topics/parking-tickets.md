# Parking tickets

*A summary of TicketCombat.com's parking-ticket section: 21 pages, written ~2008–2010,
Ontario. Not legal advice. Read the [original section introduction (archived)](https://web.archive.org/web/20251023141846/https://www.ticketcombat.com/parking/parking.php).*

> ⚠️ **Dated:** Toronto parking tickets left the court system on **Aug 28, 2017**. They now go through the city's **Administrative Penalty System (APS)**: a screening review, then a hearing officer, with no Provincial Offences Act trial. Other Ontario municipalities have adopted APS for parking too, so check your ticket. Almost everything below (trial requests, Notices of Impending Conviction, disclosure, stays and courtroom tactics) assumes the old court process. See [What's changed](../whats-changed.md).

## What this section covered

The section's argument was that a parking ticket is easier to beat than it
looks. The officer's evidence is thin, and Part II of the Provincial Offences
Act puts strict deadlines and paperwork requirements on the municipality, not
just on you. The recommended sequence was to ignore the windshield ticket, wait
for the mailed Notice of Impending Conviction, request a trial at the last
possible moment, research the by-laws behind the charge, demand disclosure,
apply for a stay if the trial took more than a year, and only then fight at
trial. Pleading guilty in court for a reduced fine was offered as a fallback.

It leaned on POA ss.15(4), 17, 17.1, 18, 18.1 and 18.1.1, Regulations 949
(parking infractions) and 497/94 (electronic documents), the HTA definitions of
parking, standing and stopping, and HTA Regulation 604. For stays it used
Charter s.7 (disclosure) and s.11(b) (delay), with *R v Morin* (1992) and
*R. v. Cassimatis* (2010). For a due diligence defence it cited *R. v. Sault
Ste. Marie* (1978) and *R. v. Kanda* (2008).

There are two layers. The site map lists only the original single-page guide,
`/offences/parking.php` (archived Dec 2008 to Feb 2010). In early 2010 it was
evidently replaced by a 20-page section under `/parking/` (first archived March
and April 2010), which the left-hand "Offences" menu linked to and the site map
never listed. Those pages are chained by previous/next arrows and a sidebar
menu, and this summary follows that order. The grouping into four parts below
is ours. The sidebar itself only sets "The Trial" in bold as the start of the
courtroom pages. Several pages illustrated their points with sample images (a
Notice of Impending Conviction, a certificate of parking infraction, an MTO
ownership certificate).

The tone is confident and adversarial. The site called its coverage the most
complete anywhere and was openly dismissive of justices' and prosecutors'
training. Its disclosure advice was tactical: it said the aim was often an
incomplete response that would support a stay. In a few places (rental cars,
borrowed pay-and-display stubs) it described ways people get around the rules
while telling readers not to be dishonest.

### How to fight your parking ticket (original 2008–2010 page)

`/offences/parking.php` · [archived copy](https://web.archive.org/web/20100210111337/http://www.ticketcombat.com/offences/parking.php) · first seen 2008-12, last captured 2010-02

The site map's only parking entry and an early, compressed version of the whole
section. It claimed Toronto didn't prosecute tickets of $30 or less once a trial
was requested (citing 2008 Toronto Star and National Post reports). It advised
waiting for the mailed Notice of Impending Conviction unless the car was rented
or borrowed, and sketched the later section's other tactics: disclosure of the
certified by-law, a delay-based stay, lost meter receipts, testing the officer's
memory in cross-examination, a moral-innocence argument about unposted time
limits, and a guilty plea for a lower fine. It also named one method to avoid,
presenting another driver's pay-and-display stub as your own, which it called
dishonest and a perjury risk.

> ⚠️ **Dated:** This page assumes a court trial and a Notice of Impending Conviction. Toronto parking tickets have gone through the city's APS since Aug 28, 2017, and other municipalities use APS too. Its delay-based stay also predates *R v Jordan*, 2016 SCC 27. See [What's changed](../whats-changed.md).

### Pleading guilty in court

`/parking/guiltyplea.php` · [archived copy](https://web.archive.org/web/20190913003852/http://www.ticketcombat.com/parking/guiltyplea.php) · first seen 2010-04, last captured 2019-09

A page on whether fighting is worth the time off work. The site said most
people simply pay, but that those who request a trial and then plead guilty in
court often get the fine sharply reduced, with no effect on the driving record
or insurance. It advised agreeing on the amount with the prosecutor before
appearing, and framed a parking trial as cheap practice for a more serious
ticket later.

> ⚠️ **Dated:** Pleading in court requires a POA trial, which Toronto parking tickets no longer get (APS since Aug 28, 2017; other municipalities use APS too). See [What's changed](../whats-changed.md).

### Ignore your parking ticket

`/parking/ignoreit.php` · [archived copy](https://web.archive.org/web/20250926195505/http://ticketcombat.com/parking/ignoreit.php) · first seen 2010-04, last captured 2025-09

Two arguments for not responding. For out-of-province plates, the site said
there was no cross-border enforcement of municipal parking fines, and cited a
high non-payment rate and Toronto leaving reminders instead of tickets (as of
~2009–2010). For Ontario plates, it read POA ss.15(4), 17 and 18 to mean that a
windshield ticket alone can't produce a default conviction. Only the Notice of
Impending Conviction mailed later can, so that notice is the document to act on.
It made exceptions for rentals and borrowed cars, where forwarding fees make it
better to request a trial within 15 days.

> ⚠️ **Dated:** The whole argument rests on the POA Notice of Impending Conviction. Toronto parking tickets have gone through APS since Aug 28, 2017, and other municipalities use APS too, so check your ticket. See [What's changed](../whats-changed.md).

### More reasons to ignore your parking ticket

`/parking/ignoreit2.php` · [archived copy](https://web.archive.org/web/20190913004922/http://www.ticketcombat.com/parking/ignoreit2.php) · first seen 2010-04, last captured 2019-09

Waiting for the Notice of Impending Conviction stretches roughly two weeks into

### Put the odds in your favour

`/parking/goododds.php` · [archived copy](https://web.archive.org/web/20190913002331/http://www.ticketcombat.com/parking/goododds.php) · first seen 2010-04, last captured 2019-09

The site called this the most important reason to ignore the ticket. It walked
through the POA Part II timeline (the response period, the window for mailing
the Notice of Impending Conviction under s.18, mail time, and another 15 days),
which it said could reach 58 days. It compared that with the 75 days the
municipality has to file its paperwork under s.18.1(2). The idea was that
using up most of that window raises the chance the city misses a deadline.

> ⚠️ **Dated:** These are POA court-process deadlines built around the Notice of Impending Conviction. Toronto parking tickets have gone through APS since Aug 28, 2017, and other municipalities use APS too. See [What's changed](../whats-changed.md).

**Part 2 — The law and the paperwork**

### Use the law to win

`/parking/quotelaw.php` · [archived copy](https://web.archive.org/web/20190913005810/http://www.ticketcombat.com/parking/quotelaw.php) · first seen 2010-04, last captured 2019-09

Advice on courtroom credibility. The site said justices and prosecutors in
parking court rarely expect legal arguments, so you should cite exact POA
sections (for example the 75-day filing rule in s.17(2)) rather than paraphrase
them. It explained the difference between, say, s.17.1 and s.17(1), and why a
wrong citation wastes time in the annotated statute books courts use.

> ⚠️ **Dated:** Written for a POA parking trial. Toronto parking tickets have gone through APS (screening, then a hearing officer) since Aug 28, 2017, and other municipalities use APS too. See [What's changed](../whats-changed.md).

### What's the charge?

`/parking/whatcharge.php` · [archived copy](https://web.archive.org/web/20190912233302/http://www.ticketcombat.com/parking/whatcharge.php) · first seen 2010-04, last captured 2019-09

This page explained the POA term "parking infraction" and the HTA definitions
of parking, standing and stopping. It noted municipal variations and exemptions
(loading time limits, funeral processions in Ottawa, accessible parking
permits, rush-hour by-laws) and HTA Regulation 604. Its main advice was to check
that the by-law cited on the ticket really covers the spot where you parked. It
retold the story of Toronto lawyer John Winegust, who parked free in spaces
next to meters that the by-laws didn't cover.

### How much is the set fine?

`/parking/setfine.php` · [archived copy](https://web.archive.org/web/20190913011035/http://www.ticketcombat.com/parking/setfine.php) · first seen 2010-04, last captured 2019-09

Parking set fines carry no victim surcharge or court costs. The site said the
province-wide default Part II fines were mostly $25 (as of ~2010), and that a
municipality charging more needs a set fine order approved by the Chief
Justice for that street, which a by-law clerk can confirm, including its
effective date. An incorrect fine, it said, could be turned into a fatal error
using the default tactic on the [fines page](your-ticket.md).

> ⚠️ **Dated:** Set fines and the default-conviction tactic belong to the POA Part II court process. Toronto parking tickets have gone through APS since Aug 28, 2017, and other municipalities use APS too, so check your ticket. See [What's changed](../whats-changed.md).

### Provincial Offences Act — intro to Part II

`/parking/poapart2.php` · [archived copy](https://web.archive.org/web/20190913011833/http://www.ticketcombat.com/parking/poapart2.php) · first seen 2010-04, last captured 2019-09

An orientation to POA Part II. The section that governs your case depends on
two things: whether you requested the trial from the windshield ticket or from
the Notice of Impending Conviction, and whether by mail or in person. That gives
four combinations, ss.17, 17.1, 18.1 and 18.1.1, shown in a small chart, and
the site said each calls for a different trial strategy. It also flagged
Regulation 949 and Regulation 497/94, the latter for electronic (thermal-paper)
tickets.

> ⚠️ **Dated:** Describes how to request a POA parking trial. Toronto parking tickets have gone through APS since Aug 28, 2017 (other municipalities too). See [What's changed](../whats-changed.md).

### Understanding the legal requirements

`/parking/proceedings.php` · [archived copy](https://web.archive.org/web/20190912224435/http://www.ticketcombat.com/parking/proceedings.php) · first seen 2010-04, last captured 2019-09

After a trial request, the municipality must "commence a proceeding" by filing
documents within 75 days of the offence (s.17(2) or s.18.1(2)). The site said
this deadline is often overlooked and should always be checked. For mail-in
requests, the certificate of parking infraction and proof of ownership must both
be filed. For in-person requests, only the certificate must be filed up front,
but ownership evidence, the certificate and a copy of the notice of trial must
be produced at trial (s.17.1(7), applied through s.18.1.1(2.1)).

> ⚠️ **Dated:** The filing rules govern POA parking prosecutions. Toronto parking tickets have gone through APS since Aug 28, 2017, and other municipalities use APS too. See [What's changed](../whats-changed.md).

### Certificate of parking infraction

`/parking/certificate.php` · [archived copy](https://web.archive.org/web/20250815112155/http://www.ticketcombat.com/parking/certificate.php) · first seen 2010-04, last captured 2025-08

The site said this was the most misunderstood document in parking court. The
ticket you receive is a Parking Infraction Notice. The certificate is a separate
document filed in court, in which the officer certifies that the ticket was
served by attaching it to the car or handing it over. If you drive away before
service, no valid certificate can be completed and no proceeding can start. It
contrasted this with Part I tickets, where the driver's copy and the court's
certificate are identical carbon copies.

> ⚠️ **Dated:** Concerns evidence in a POA parking trial. Toronto parking tickets have gone through APS since Aug 28, 2017 (other municipalities too). See [What's changed](../whats-changed.md).

**Part 3 — Before trial: disclosure and stays**

### Disclosure

`/parking/parkingdisclosure.php` · [archived copy](https://web.archive.org/web/20190913002221/http://www.ticketcombat.com/parking/parkingdisclosure.php) · first seen 2010-04, last captured 2019-09

The site argued that because ss.17(3), 17.1(7), 18.1(3) and 18.1.1(2.1)
describe the certificate and ownership proof as "evidence", they are subject to
disclosure. Requesting them is the only way to check whether the proceeding was
started properly. It offered a parking-specific request form (see Downloads) and
discussed openly how much to ask for: listing every item shows diligence, but a
complete response would remove grounds for a stay. It left the choice to the
reader's judgment of their municipality.

> ⚠️ **Dated:** Parking disclosure is part of the POA court process. Toronto parking tickets have gone through APS since Aug 28, 2017, and other municipalities use APS too, so check your ticket. See [What's changed](../whats-changed.md).

### Stay your charge

`/parking/parkingstay.php` · [archived copy](https://web.archive.org/web/20250818100225/http://ticketcombat.com/parking/parkingstay.php) · first seen 2010-04, last captured 2025-08

Two Charter grounds for a stay. Under s.7, the site argued that failing to
disclose the original ticket (which, in cities like Toronto, may record make
and model details missing from other documents) harms your defence. Under
s.11(b), it argued that a parking trial should happen within about a year,
because the 75-day filing limit caps intake time and *R v Morin* (1992)
suggested 8–10 months of institutional delay. Its example was *R. v.
Cassimatis* (2010), where a defendant with two unanswered disclosure requests
and a trial about 15 months after his trial request got a stay. The same
justice had decided *R. v. Rowan* (2004), a speeding case.

> ⚠️ **Dated:** Toronto parking tickets have gone through APS, not court, since Aug 28, 2017 (other municipalities too). The 11(b) reasoning comes from the Askov/Morin era, which *R v Jordan*, 2016 SCC 27 replaced with presumptive ceilings (18 months in provincial court). See [What's changed](../whats-changed.md).

### Photographic evidence

`/parking/photos.php` · [archived copy](https://web.archive.org/web/20190913000047/http://www.ticketcombat.com/parking/photos.php) · first seen 2010-04, last captured 2019-09

Some cities had given parking officers digital cameras. The site's response was
two-fold: object if the photos weren't disclosed before trial, then challenge
their admissibility. It described what it considered the authentication
requirements: original media or RAW files, an unbroken chain of custody,
sequential file numbering, and notes on camera settings, since brightening a
night shot can misrepresent how visible a sign was. It ended with a reading list,
including *R. c. MacNeil*, 2008 QCCS 915.

> ⚠️ **Dated:** Assumes a POA court trial. Toronto parking tickets have gone through APS since Aug 28, 2017 (other municipalities too). See [What's changed](../whats-changed.md).

### Cross-examining the officer

`/parking/crossexam.php` · [archived copy](https://web.archive.org/web/20190913002524/http://www.ticketcombat.com/parking/crossexam.php) · first seen 2010-04, last captured 2019-09

A parking officer usually knows the street and its signs very well, so the site
advised against challenging that. Instead, probe what they remember about the
day and your car, to show that they are only reading a plate number from their
notes. The aim is to chip away at reliability, not win outright. For meters, it
repeated that the lack of a receipt leaves your word against theirs.

> ⚠️ **Dated:** Assumes a POA court trial. Toronto parking tickets have gone through APS since Aug 28, 2017 (other municipalities too). See [What's changed](../whats-changed.md).

### Morally innocent (strict liability)

`/parking/strict.php` · [archived copy](https://web.archive.org/web/20190913001236/http://www.ticketcombat.com/parking/strict.php) · first seen 2010-04, last captured 2019-09

Relying on *R. v. Sault Ste. Marie* (1978) and *R. v. Kanda* (2008), the site
argued that parking infractions are strict liability offences, so a due
diligence defence is available, and that justices often wrongly treat them as
absolute liability. Its examples were a driver who checked for signs and
reasonably missed one, and Toronto's default three-hour limit, which it said
was not posted at city entrances the way Mississauga's was.

> ⚠️ **Dated:** Written as a defence at a POA court trial. Toronto parking tickets have gone through APS since Aug 28, 2017 (other municipalities too). See [What's changed](../whats-changed.md).

## Key takeaways

- The site's core idea: under POA Part II the municipality, not just the driver,
  faces strict deadlines and document rules, and those can be used against it.
- Its signature tactic was to ignore the windshield ticket and act only on the
  mailed Notice of Impending Conviction, as late as possible.
- It treated the certificate of parking infraction, MTO-certified ownership
  proof and the notice of trial as the minimum evidence, and built disclosure,
  stay and non-suit arguments around them.
- At trial it favoured legal arguments and careful cross-examination over the
  driver's own testimony, with a due diligence argument as a backup.
- A negotiated guilty plea in court was the low-effort alternative.
- For Toronto since Aug 28, 2017, and for other municipalities on APS, the
  court-based strategy no longer applies, so check your ticket first.
