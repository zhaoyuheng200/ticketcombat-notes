# Your ticket

*A summary of TicketCombat.com's "Your Ticket" pages: 5 pages, written ~2008–2012,
Ontario. Not legal advice. Start with the [first page, on demerit points (archived)](https://web.archive.org/web/20251020144123/https://www.ticketcombat.com/offences/demeritpoints.php).*

## What this section covered

These were reference pages, not steps. They sat under a "Your Ticket" heading in
the site's left-hand menu (labelled Your Options, Demerit Points, Insurance Hike,
Fines and Fatal Errors), and the site map grouped them under "Understanding Your
Ticket". The first menu item, "Your Options", is the Step 1 introduction and is
covered in [Step 1 — Fight the ticket](../guide/step-1-fight-the-ticket.md). The
other four pages live under `/offences/`, and a fifth, a speeding set-fine table,
is linked only from the fines page.

Together they set out what a conviction actually costs: demerit points under
Regulation 339 of the Highway Traffic Act, the much longer and larger cost of
higher insurance premiums, and the arithmetic behind the "total payable" printed
on a ticket (set fine, victim fine surcharge, court costs). The fines and fatal
errors pages then turn that arithmetic into a defence. They separate errors a
justice can fix under POA s.34 from errors that end a case, and describe a
riskier tactic, based on *London (City) v. Young*, 2008 ONCA 429, for making a
wrong fine amount fatal.

The tone is plainer than the rest of the site. These pages are mostly tables
and explanations, with the site's usual suspicion of ticket revenue and of
prosecutors showing through in the fines and fatal-error pages. All dollar
amounts, point values and insurance percentages are as of when each page was
written (mostly ~2008–2009; the speeding table ~2012).

## Pages

### Demerit points and what they mean

`/offences/demeritpoints.php` · [archived copy](https://web.archive.org/web/20251020144123/https://www.ticketcombat.com/offences/demeritpoints.php) · first seen 2008-12, last captured 2025-10

A reference page on the demerit system under HTA Regulation 339. It stressed
that drivers accumulate points rather than lose them, that points count for two
years while the conviction stays on the driver's abstract for three, and that
the thresholds for a warning letter, a Ministry interview and an automatic
30-day suspension are much lower for novice (G1, G2, M1, M2) drivers than for
fully licensed ones. Most of the page is the offence-by-offence points schedule,
from 7 points (e.g. failing to remain at an accident) down to 2 (e.g. seat belt
and signalling offences) (values as of ~2008).

### Insurance implications of your ticket

`/offences/insurancehit.php` · [archived copy](https://web.archive.org/web/20251018150733/https://www.ticketcombat.com/offences/insurancehit.php) · first seen 2008-12, last captured 2025-10

The site argued that insurance, not the fine, is the real cost of a moving
violation. Even a no-points conviction goes on the abstract, while parking
tickets do not. It was sceptical of insurers' first-conviction forgiveness
("rate protector") clauses, saying you pay for them in advance and lose them for
the next ticket. It estimated that
a small ticket could add hundreds of dollars a year for five years or more, and
included tables of premium increases by number of minor, major and serious
convictions, with lists of which offences fall in each class (figures from
~2008–2009). It also covered how long records are kept (three years at the
Ministry, typically five for insurers), at-fault accidents under the Fault
Determination Rules, and the Facility Association as the insurer of last
resort.

### Fines explained, how they are calculated and which ones apply

`/offences/fines.php` · [archived copy](https://web.archive.org/web/20251018230950/https://www.ticketcombat.com/offences/fines.php) · first seen 2008-12, last captured 2025-10

This page broke a fine into its parts: how you were charged (POA Part I, II or
III, with the Part I cap raised from $500 to $1,000 by the Good Government Act),
the set fine from the Ontario Court of Justice schedules or the statutory amount
(noting that the Schedule 43 speeding rates differ from those in HTA s.128(14)),
the victim fine surcharge under the Victims' Bill of Rights and O. Reg. 161/00
(not charged on parking tickets, POA s.60.1), and court costs under Regulation
945. Its main claim was that a miscalculated fine is harmless at trial, where
the justice simply corrects it. But under *London (City) v. Young* it becomes
fatal if you do nothing and let the ticket go to a default conviction, because
on a default the justice must check the certificate under POA s.9(1)(b) and
quash it if it is defective on its face. The site flagged the risk (the justice
may not check) and said an appeal was the fallback (amounts as of ~2008–2009).

### Speeding set fines (table)

`/offences/speeding_setfine.php` · [archived copy](https://web.archive.org/web/20200220024107/http://www.ticketcombat.com/offences/speeding_setfine.php) · first seen 2012-10, last captured 2020-02

A single table giving the set fine, victim surcharge, $5 cost and total payable
for each km/h over the limit from 1 to 49, with 50 and over marked as
summons-only. It was built from the Schedule 43 set fines and shows the jumps at
20 and 30 km/h over (figures as of ~2012). (Not in the site map or menu; linked
only from the fines page as its complete list of speeding fines.)

### Fatal errors, what are they, how do you use it to your advantage

`/offences/fatal.php` · [archived copy](https://web.archive.org/web/20251021221033/https://www.ticketcombat.com/offences/fatal.php) · first seen 2008-12, last captured 2025-10

The site separated ticket mistakes a justice can amend under POA s.34 (such as a
misspelled name, wrong plate, wrong time or wrong fine) from defects it said
would end the case (such as a missing offence date, name or location, no officer
signature, a non-existent offence, or filing more than seven days late). It
warned against pointing out an error to the prosecutor before trial or at the
pre-trial meeting, since that only lets them fix it, or withdraw and re-lay the
charge by summons within six months. It pointed to Step 5's "quash the trial"
page and the default tactic on the fines page for more.

> ⚠️ **Dated:** Ontario's online portal now lets you request an early resolution meeting (the meeting the site calls First Attendance) or a trial, where the court offers it. See [What's changed](../whats-changed.md).

## Key takeaways

- The site's case for fighting rested on cost: a conviction's insurance impact
  outlasts and outweighs the fine and the demerit points.
- Demerit points are added, not deducted, and the warning and suspension
  thresholds are much lower for novice drivers.
- The "total payable" is set fine + victim surcharge + court costs. Parking
  tickets carry no surcharge.
- An error on a ticket is usually fixable at trial. The site's list of truly
  fatal defects was short.
- Its tactic of defaulting to make a wrong fine fatal (*London (City) v. Young*,
  2008) was presented as risky, with an appeal as the backup.
- Every number on these pages (points, fines, insurance percentages) is a
  snapshot from ~2008–2012.
